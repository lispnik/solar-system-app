Privacy Policy

Where the Planets Are · 21 September 2026

The app collects nothing, and nothing leaves your phone. It has no account, no advertising, no analytics and no third-party software in it. It makes no network requests at all: every position it draws is computed on the phone from tables built into the app.

Your location

If you press the location button, the app asks iOS where you are, so that it can show the sky as it looks from there — the horizon, the compass points, and how high each planet stands above them. That location is used while the app is running and is never stored, never written to a file, and never sent anywhere. Turning the button off, or refusing the permission, stops the app asking; the rest of it works either way.

Motion

When you hold the phone up to the sky, the app reads which way the phone is facing from iOS's motion sensors, to turn the view with it. Those readings are used as they arrive and are not kept.

What is kept on the phone

So that the app opens where you left it, it saves a few settings in the phone's own storage: the date you were looking at, the speed, which toggles were on, where the camera pointed and what it was following. This stays on your phone, belongs to the app, and is deleted with it.

Children

Nothing is collected from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If a future version of the app ever collects anything, this page will say so before that version is released.

Questions

The app is open source. You can read exactly what it does, and raise anything you find, at github.com/lispnik/solar-system-app — questions and problems are welcome in its issues.
